# Business Validation (Days 1–2)

The weakest part of this project is proving the problem is real. The whole value
proposition rests on the claim that credentials are painful to re-verify when
workers and equipment move between projects. If that pain is not real or not
large, we have a technically impressive solution to a problem nobody has — and
graders will push on exactly this.

This is a **first-class deliverable**, not a side task. Two people own it on
Days 1–2, in parallel with the technical leads freezing the architecture. The
output is a decision made by **end of Day 2**: validated pain, or defensible
reframe. Do not defer this to Day 14.

---

## Owner & timebox

- Owners: Person 5 (business/governance) + one other.
- Timebox: Days 1–2 only. Decide and move on.
- Deliverable: one short slide/section stating the chosen position + evidence.

---

## Path A — validate the pain (preferred)

Find documented evidence that re-verification / re-submission of credentials
between projects is a real, repeated cost. Look at:

- [ ] WSH Council (Singapore) guidance on lifting operations and competency
- [ ] MOM requirements for crane operators, lifting supervisors, riggers, signalmen
- [ ] BCA / contractor onboarding or pre-qualification checklists
- [ ] Authorised Examiner (AE) regime for lifting equipment inspection
- [ ] Permit-to-Work (PTW) preparation steps in existing e-PTW systems
- [ ] Any public tender/contract doc listing credential submission requirements
- [ ] One short interview with a practitioner (safety officer, PM, PTW applicant)

Capture for each source: what it says, the link/citation, and how strongly it
supports "re-verification between projects is a repeated pain."

**If confirmed:** lock the motivation around reduced admin, faster onboarding,
fewer re-submissions, and fewer expired/forged credentials slipping through.

---

## Path B — defensible reframe (fallback)

If the admin-pain evidence is weak, pivot to a claim that stands on its own and
does not depend on quantifying admin cost:

> The value is **independent verifiability and tamper-evidence** of safety-critical
> credentials across parties with no shared trust boundary. No single contractor
> or software vendor owns the master record, and anyone authorised can
> independently verify that a credential was not forged or silently revoked.

This is defensible on its face: it is about *trust and integrity across
organizations*, not *saving minutes of admin*. The blockchain layer is justified
by the multi-party trust problem regardless of admin volume.
